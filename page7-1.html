Our research question for this experiment was “What substance melts ice the fastest?”, and our hypothesis was that salt would melt the ice the fastest.

We started this experiment because we were curious about why people use salt to melt ice during winter. For the independent variables, we chose salt (our hypothesis), sugar, soda, and pure water.

The equipment used in our experiment included a 500 ml measuring cylinder, 50 ml beaker cups, lab coats, goggles, and gloves.

To improve the accuracy of the experiment, we divided it into three parts: A, B, and C.

Our overall method was to use the same amount of water and substances in each trial and to keep

the amount and size of the ice cubes the same (control variables). After that, we dropped similar-sized ice cubes into each substance at the same time and started the timer.

The dependent variable was the time taken for the ice to melt.

First, in Experiment A, we tested three different temperatures of water: hot water, cold water, and room-temperature water. From this experiment, we found that hot water melted the ice the fastest, followed by room-temperature water, and then cold water.

In Experiment B, we compared different states of matter: water, air, and salt (solid). The results showed that room-temperature water melted the ice the fastest, followed by salt, and then plain ice.

Finally, in Experiment C, we tested four different substances: room-temperature water, salt, soda, and sugar. The results showed that room-temperature water melted the ice the fastest, followed by salt, soda, and sugar.

Overall, hot water was the most effective way to melt ice, taking only 1 minute and 55 seconds. This disproved our hypothesis because hot water melted the ice faster than salt.
