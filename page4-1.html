Method 1

1. Prepare three identical containers and three ice cubes of the same shape and size.

2. Place one ice cube into each container, making a total of three pairs.

3. Before using the electronic kettle to boil the water, wear safety gloves to prevent burn.

4. Prepare water at three different temperatures using an electric kettle. Cold water (approximately 3–7 degrees)

Lukewarm water (approximately 20–25 degrees) (Adjust the temperature as closely as possible to the room temperature for easy comparison in other experiments) Warm water (60–65 degrees)

*Originally, we intended to prepare lukewarm, warm, and very hot water, but we changed the alternative to cold water to prevent burns.

5. To ensure accurate measurement, pour the water into the containers using a 500ml measuring cylinder. (Fill the containers with water just enough to barely cover the ice.)

6. After pouring those water into containers, by using a thermometer to check the right temperature.

7 . Use a stopwatch to time the ice until it turns into water, then compare which temperature of water condition took the shortest amount of time to melt the ice.

Method 2

1. Prepare three identical containers and three ice cubes of the same shape and size.

2. Place one ice cube into each container, making a total of three pairs.

3. Prepare salt, but wear eye protection(safety goggles) to prevent the powder from getting into your eyes.

4. Place an ice cube coated evenly with salt (ensuring it is well sealed off from air) into one container (use a spatula to thoroughly coat the ice with the solid).

5. In another container, use a measuring cylinder (for accurate measurement) to pour room-temperature water (approximately 20 to 25 degrees) over the ice cube until it barely covers the ice.

6. The third container will contain nothing but ice, and only the room temperature will be used to melt the ice (meaning the ice will be melted using only air).

7. Place the three containers side by side in a visible location and time them with a stopwatch to compare which one took the least amount of time to melt the ice.

Method 3

1. Prepare four identical containers and four ice cubes of the same shape and size.

2. Place one ice cube into each container, making a total of four pairs this time.

3. Prepare salt, sugar, water(room temperature), and soda drink (also room temperature) but wear eye protection(safety goggles) to prevent the splash from getting into your eyes.

4. Dissolve salt and sugar in room temperature water to make salt water and sugar water. (Make them in a 1:1 ratio.) A measuring cylinder and a spatula will be used here for accurate measurement and stirring.

Also, bring the carbonated water and regular water to room temperature. At this stage, the volume of all liquids must be identical.

5. Transfer everything prepared into designated containers.

Pour the solutions only enough to barely cover the ice.

Container 1 - Salt water (Salt NaCl)

Container 2 - Sugar water (Sugar C12H22O11)

Container 3 - Room temperature carbonated water (Carbon dioxide CO2)

Container 4 - Room temperature water (Pure water H2O with nothing added)

6. Place the containers side by side in a visible location and time them using a stopwatch.

7. Afterward, compare the solutions to determine which one melted the ice the fastest.
